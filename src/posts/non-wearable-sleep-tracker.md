Some people notice a ring or watch all night, pull it off at 3 a.m., or forget to charge it and lose the night's data anyway. If that sounds like you, but you'd still like a rough picture of how you're sleeping, a non-wearable sleep tracker is the obvious place to look.

There are two main kinds. One sits under your mattress and picks up movement through it. The other sits on your nightstand and senses you from a short distance. Which one makes sense depends less on the spec sheet and more on your bed, who shares it, and what you're willing to put next to your pillow.

Below, we explain what they can tell you, how to choose a type, and our main picks. If you're fine with wearing something to bed after all, our guide to a [smart ring with no subscription]({{ '/posts/smart-ring-no-subscription-oura-alternative/' | url }}) covers that route instead.

## What a Non-Wearable Sleep Tracker Can and Can't Tell You

### How They Measure Without Touching You

An under-mattress pad works by sensing the small movements your body makes while you lie on it. Withings, for example, describes its Sleep pad as tracking sleep stages, heart rate during sleep, snoring and what it calls "breathing disturbances," all from under the mattress. Nothing touches your skin.

Bedside sensors use low-power radar instead. Google says the Nest Hub (2nd gen) uses its Motion Sense radar to detect movement and breathing, and uses its microphone and other sensors for sound, light and temperature in the room. Sleepal says its AI Lamp combines radar with an infrared (thermal) sensor to follow movement and breathing. Neither device has a camera, according to their makers.

In both cases, the device is estimating sleep from indirect signals.

### What You Give Up Compared With a Ring or Watch

Not wearing anything has costs:

- **No daytime data.** A pad or a bedside sensor knows nothing about your steps, workouts or daytime heart rate.
- **It only works in one place.** Naps on the couch, nights at a hotel and sleep on a plane aren't recorded.
- **Other people and pets can get in the way.** A ring follows one person. A device that watches a bed or a room can be confused by whoever else is in it.

If those trade-offs bother you, our [Oura Ring vs. Whoop comparison]({{ '/posts/oura-ring-vs-whoop-sleep-tracking/' | url }}) and our [Apple Watch vs. Oura Ring for sleep]({{ '/posts/apple-watch-vs-oura-ring-sleep-tracking/' | url }}) guide look at wearables that track around the clock.

### Wellness Trackers, Not Diagnostic Tools

The consumer products in this guide are sold for general wellness. Withings describes the U.S. version of Sleep as a wellness device, separate from its prescription-only Sleep Rx. Google says Sleep Sensing isn't intended to diagnose, treat or prevent any disease or condition. We couldn't find a statement either way from Sleepal, so we don't describe its features in medical terms at all.

Read any of these as a trend over weeks, not a verdict on a single night.

### If You Share the Bed

Both types are built around one sleeper. One Withings pad covers one person, so two people need two pads. Google says the Nest Hub tracks the person closest to the display, and that other people or pets nearby can affect the data.

If the real problem in your bed is a partner's snoring rather than your own sleep data, our guide to [sleep earbuds vs. earplugs for a snoring partner]({{ '/posts/sleep-earbuds-vs-earplugs-snoring-partner/' | url }}) may help more than a tracker.

### If You Have an Airbed, Waterbed, or Adjustable Base

Withings says its sleep pad works with spring, latex, foam and memory foam mattresses, and that it was tested on mattresses roughly 4 to 15 inches thick. It does not recommend waterbeds, airbeds or mattresses that vibrate. On slatted frames, Withings suggests putting a flat board, even a piece of cardboard, under the pad. We couldn't find an official statement about adjustable bases, so if you have one, check with Withings before you buy.

A bedside sensor doesn't depend on the mattress, so it's simpler for unusual beds.

### If Bedside Light or a Screen Bothers You

A pad disappears under the mattress. Bedside sensors don't: the Nest Hub has a screen, and the Sleepal AI Lamp is a lamp. Sleepal says its lamp dims at bedtime, but if any light by the bed bothers you, a pad is the safer choice.

### Withings Sleep: An Under-Mattress Pad You Set Up Once

**What it's for:** people who want sleep tracking with nothing on their body and nothing new on the nightstand.

Withings Sleep is a thin pad that goes under your mattress, or between the mattress and a topper; if you can feel it, Withings suggests moving it between the mattress and the bed frame. Outside the U.S., a similar product is sold as "Sleep Analyzer," and the two don't have the same features.

The pad runs on a power adapter and stays plugged into an outlet. Setup needs Wi-Fi, and Withings lists iOS 16 or later and Android 12 or later. One pad covers one person.

According to Withings, the pad tracks sleep stages, gives a Sleep Score, and records heart rate during sleep and snoring. It also reports breathing disturbances, but only under two conditions Withings spells out: you need at least five hours of sleep, and the pad's audio sensor has to be turned on.

**What it is not.** The product names here are easy to mix up:

- Withings describes the U.S. Withings Sleep as a wellness device. It does not include sleep apnea detection. That feature belongs to the Sleep Analyzer sold in some other regions.
- A separate product, Withings Sleep Rx, received FDA 510(k) clearance in 2024 as an aid in diagnosing obstructive sleep apnea. It's intended for use through healthcare providers and isn't the consumer pad you'd buy for your own bedroom.
- Withings says the U.S. Sleep hasn't yet been tested for use during CPAP treatment.

The consumer pad won't tell you whether you have sleep apnea. If you're worried about that, talk to a doctor.

**Subscription.** Withings+ is an optional paid plan. Withings says you can see your sleep history and trends without it.

**Who it may not suit:**

- People with an airbed or a waterbed, which Withings doesn't recommend.
- Couples who want both people tracked with a single pad. You'd need two.
- Anyone without an outlet near the bed.
- People who want to take their tracker on trips. The pad is designed to stay put.
- People using CPAP, since Withings says the U.S. version hasn't yet been tested for that.

### Sleepal AI Lamp: A Bedside Lamp That Tracks Sleep

**What it's for:** people who want one bedside device that handles light, a wake-up alarm and sleep tracking.

The Sleepal AI Lamp is a newer product, shown at CES 2026. Sleepal says it uses radar and an infrared (thermal) sensor, without a camera, and has a touch privacy control that physically disconnects the sensors at the circuit level.

On sleep, Sleepal lists time to fall asleep, sleep stages, sleep position and snoring. It also records the bedroom itself: temperature, humidity, sound and light. As a lamp, it dims and can wake you with light and sound. If the wake-up light is the part that appeals most, our [sunrise alarm clock comparison]({{ '/posts/sunrise-alarm-clocks-wake-up-light-comparison/' | url }}) covers devices built mainly for that.

Sleepal lists Apple Health and Google Health Connect support, plus Matter for Apple Home and Google Home. It uses 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz Wi-Fi and lists iOS 16.4+ and Android 7.0+. Its site links to the App Store and Google Play, and also offers an Android APK download.

**Subscription.** Sleepal says the lamp works without a subscription. Its AI+ tier is optional, and the site mentions a subscription period included with purchase without making clear which tier it covers. Check the current terms, since they may change.

Sleepal publishes its own accuracy figures; we don't repeat them, because they come from the company rather than an independent source. Because the product is new, there isn't much long-term owner feedback yet, so read recent reviews and the return policy first.

**Who it may not suit:**

- People who don't want any light source next to the bed.
- Shoppers who prefer a product with years of owner reviews.
- Anyone looking for the least expensive option. This is a premium device.

## FAQ

**Can an under-mattress sleep tracker detect sleep apnea?**

Not the consumer Withings Sleep sold in the U.S., which Withings describes as a wellness device and which doesn't include sleep apnea detection. A separate product for healthcare providers, Withings Sleep Rx, received FDA clearance as an aid in diagnosing obstructive sleep apnea. If you're concerned about your breathing during sleep, talk to a doctor.

**Do non-wearable sleep trackers work for couples?**

Mostly one person per device. Two people need two devices, and partners or pets nearby can affect the data.

**Do I need a subscription?**

Not for core tracking, according to the makers. Withings says sleep history and trends are available without Withings+. Sleepal says the lamp works without a subscription, with AI+ as an option. Google offers Sleep Sensing as a no-extra-cost preview but has mentioned that could change.

**Are non-wearable trackers as accurate as a ring or watch?**

We don't know of enough independent public data to say. Different devices can give different results for the same night, so watch the trend over weeks.

**Does the Nest Hub have a camera?**

No. Google says the Nest Hub (2nd gen) has no camera; Sleep Sensing uses radar and other sensors.

**What happened to the Amazon Halo Rise?**

Amazon discontinued its Halo devices in 2023 and ended the service, so the Halo Rise no longer works.

## A Note on Health and Accuracy

This article is for general informational purposes only and is not medical advice. The consumer devices here are made for wellness tracking; their numbers are estimates, and they can't diagnose sleep disorders.

Talk to a doctor if you snore loudly, if someone has told you that you stop breathing or gasp during sleep, if you feel very sleepy during the day, or if trouble sleeping has lasted several weeks. Don't change any medication or treatment based on what a sleep tracker shows.
