Weighted blankets are one of the few sleep-wellness products that graduated from "trendy" to "normal household item" without fading out — a good sign for a product's staying power, and for anyone writing about it long-term.

## What the research actually shows

The idea behind weighted blankets is deep pressure stimulation — a gentle, even pressure across the body that some research links to reduced physiological arousal (lower heart rate, calmer nervous system response). Several small studies have found modest improvements in self-reported sleep quality and anxiety symptoms among users, though sample sizes are often small and results aren't universal. It's not a guaranteed fix, and it isn't a substitute for treatment of clinical anxiety or sleep disorders — but for general stress-related restlessness, the effect is plausible and low-risk to try.

## How to pick the right weight

The most common recommendation is choosing a blanket that's roughly 10% of your body weight, give or take a pound or two.

| Body weight | Suggested blanket weight |
|---|---|
| 100–130 lbs | 10–13 lbs |
| 130–170 lbs | 13–17 lbs |
| 170–200 lbs | 17–20 lbs |
| 200+ lbs | 20–25 lbs |

Couples sharing a bed usually either size for the lighter partner or buy two individual blankets rather than one oversized one — a single heavy blanket sized for two people gets uncomfortably hot and hard to move under.

## Material matters more than most buyers expect

- **Cotton/cooling weave**: Best for hot sleepers or warmer climates — breathable, less heat retention.
- **Minky/plush**: Softer feel, but traps more heat — better for cooler bedrooms.
- **Glass bead fill**: The standard weighting method; distributes evenly and doesn't shift as much as plastic pellets.

## Who this helps most — and who it won't

Weighted blankets tend to help most with general restlessness, racing thoughts at bedtime, and the "can't settle down" feeling after a stressful day. They're not a treatment for insomnia caused by an underlying medical condition, and they're not recommended for young children, or anyone with certain respiratory or circulatory conditions, without checking with a doctor first.

If you're a hot sleeper, get the cooling-weave version or you'll likely stop using it within a month — that's the single most common reason people abandon weighted blankets, more than the weight itself.
