## Why Massage Guns Are Loud in an Apartment

### Two Kinds of Noise: The Buzz You Hear and the Thump That Travels

The motor and the moving head create a buzz that travels through the air. That's what most people mean by "loud," and it's what a decibel rating tries to describe.

The second kind is easier to overlook. Each stroke of the head is a small impact, and if your body is resting on a bed frame, a headboard, or a hard floor, some of that energy may pass into the structure. Sound that travels through the building itself can reach people who can't hear the motor at all, much like footsteps from the apartment above. In our guide to [white noise machines for apartment noise]({{ '/posts/white-noise-machine-for-apartment-noise/' | url }}), we cover why footsteps and thumps are the hardest kind of apartment noise to deal with. The same idea may apply here, just in the other direction.

### What Changes the Sound: Speed, Pressure, Attachment, and Surface

Several things you control may change how loud a massage gun seems:

- **Speed.** Higher settings mean more strokes per minute, and the sound may rise with them.
- **Pressure.** Pressing harder can load the motor and change its pitch. Some devices may also get louder when pushed into the body.
- **Attachment.** A hard, small head and a softer, wider head may sound different against the body.
- **What's underneath you.** A thick rug or a yoga mat may absorb more vibration than a bare floor or a wooden bed frame.

None of this shows up on a spec sheet.

## How to Read a Massage Gun's Decibel Rating

### Few Brands Publish a Number

Among the manufacturer pages we checked for this guide, Ekrin was the only brand that listed a decibel range on its own site. Its site lists 35–55 dB for the B37 and 35–58 dB for the Bantam.

The other brands we looked at use words instead: "whisper-quiet" for the Theragun Relief, QuietGlide technology for the Hypervolt Go 2, and ultra-quiet for the Bob and Brad Q2 Mini. Those may be accurate, but they aren't numbers you can compare.

### Why a Range Like "35–55 dB" Isn't a Promise

A published range is useful, but it has limits worth keeping in mind:

- **The conditions aren't published.** Ekrin doesn't state how far away the measurement was taken, at which speed, or whether the head was pressed against anything. The low end of the range is likely to reflect a low speed, but we can't confirm that.
- **You can't compare it with other brands.** Different companies may measure in different ways. Without the conditions, a lower number from one brand doesn't prove that device is quieter than another brand's.
- **Your room is not a test room.** Distance, wall thickness, furniture, and the surface under you can all change what someone else actually hears.

So we treat a published range as one piece of information, not a ranking, and we don't name a single "quietest" model.

**A quick spec checklist for apartment use:**

- Does the manufacturer publish a decibel figure, and does it say how it was measured?
- How much does it weigh? You may hold it for several minutes at a time.
- How long does the battery last, according to the manufacturer?
- Is a soft or cushioned attachment included?
- Is the amplitude (how far the head travels on each stroke) published?

## How to Keep the Noise Down at Night

These steps may reduce how much sound and vibration reaches other people, though results depend on your building and your device.

### Start on the Lowest Speed and a Soft Attachment

Begin on the lowest setting and only go up if you need to. If your device includes a softer head, such as the Dampener on the Theragun Relief, try that first. Let the device rest on the area rather than pressing it in hard, since heavy pressure may change the motor's sound.

### Mind What's Underneath You

Where you sit may matter as much as which device you own. Try to:

- Avoid leaning against a headboard, a bed frame, or a wall you share with a neighbor.
- Sit on a rug, a yoga mat, or a padded chair rather than a bare hard floor.
- Use the device in a room that doesn't share a wall with a bedroom, if you can.
- Keep the device from touching furniture or the floor while it's running.

### Pick Your Timing and Tell the Household

A short session before someone settles down may cause less disruption than one just as they're falling asleep. Agreeing on a time with your partner or roommates may help more than any spec. Building quiet hours vary, so check your lease or building rules.

## FAQ

**What is the quietest massage gun?**

We can't name one. Manufacturers measure noise under different conditions, and many don't publish a figure at all. Among the brands we checked, Ekrin was the only one with a published decibel range on its own site: 35–55 dB for the B37 and 35–58 dB for the Bantam, with no measurement conditions stated.

**Is 55 dB quiet enough to use while someone is sleeping?**

There isn't a single answer. What reaches a sleeping person depends on distance, the walls between you, and any vibration traveling through the floor or bed. Using the lowest speed in a separate room may matter more than the number itself.

**Are mini massage guns quieter than full-size ones?**

Not necessarily. Ekrin's compact Bantam is listed at 35–58 dB, a slightly higher upper figure than its full-size B37 at 35–55 dB. Size alone doesn't tell you much about noise.

**Can neighbors hear a massage gun through the wall or floor?**

They may. The motor's buzz travels through the air, but the strokes can also pass vibration into a bed frame, a wall, or the floor. Using the device away from shared walls, on a rug or mat, may reduce what reaches them.

**Why does my massage gun get louder over time?**

For some Theragun models, Therabody's FAQ describes a maintenance step using the QuietForce Solution included in the box if the device gets noisier with use. Other brands have their own guidance, so check your manual or contact the manufacturer.
