## Why a Sound Machine's Light Can Bother You at Night

Some people don't notice a small light at all; others find it hard to ignore once they've seen it. We can't tell you how much a small light affects anyone's sleep, but we can help you spot where the light on a sound machine comes from.

### Power LEDs, Clock Displays, Night Lights, and Charging Lights

Light on a sound machine usually comes from one of four places:

- **Power indicator LEDs.** A small dot that shows the machine is on. Some stay lit the whole time the machine is running.
- **Clock displays.** Common on combined alarm-clock sound machines. Some can be dimmed, some can be turned off, and some can't.
- **Night lights.** A soft glow meant for late-night trips to the bathroom or for a child's room. Some have their own on/off control.
- **Charging lights.** On machines with a battery, a light may show while charging or when the battery is low.

A listing that says "no night light" may still have a power LED, and one that says the display turns off may not mention a charging light. Check each source separately.

### "Dimmable" Is Not the Same as "Off"

Many listings say a display or button light is "dimmable" or has "adjustable brightness." That's useful, but it only tells you the light can get dimmer. It doesn't tell you whether the lowest setting is fully off. If you want a dark room, look for wording that says the light turns off, goes dark, or can be disabled, not only that it dims.

## Sound Machine, No Lights: How to Read a Listing

Before you buy, run the listing and the manufacturer's page through this checklist:

- [ ] **Is there a light besides the night light?** Look for any mention of a power light, status light, or indicator. Check the product photos with the machine switched on, if there are any.
- [ ] **Can the light be turned off, and how?** A button on the machine, a setting in an app, or not at all. If it needs an app, you'll need your phone set up before bedtime.
- [ ] **Does it light up again when you touch it?** Some displays come back on for a few seconds when you press a button or tap the top.
- [ ] **Does it glow while charging?** For battery models, the manual is often the only place this shows up.
- [ ] **Does the listing only say "dimmable"?** If so, assume it may not go fully dark until the manufacturer says otherwise.

If the manufacturer doesn't say, treat it as unknown rather than as "no light."

## How to Cut the Glow From Any Sound Machine

**Use the machine's own settings first.** Check the manual for a built-in way to turn lights off. On the Loftie Clock, Blackout Mode (press and hold snooze) turns the display off, a single press of snooze toggles the night light, and Red Mode is another display option Loftie offers. On a Hatch Restore 3, Hatch's quickstart guide says the clock display can be hidden. On a Dohm Nova, the Light button turns the night light on and off. Built-in settings don't involve covering anything, so start there.

**Move it out of your line of sight.** Placement can make a big difference:

- Turn the lit side away from the bed, toward the wall.
- Place it lower than your head, such as on a lower shelf or the floor beside the nightstand, as long as that's a stable surface.
- Put it behind something, like a book or lamp base, that blocks the line between the light and your eyes.
- Leave space around it, and don't block any air openings.

**If you use tape, use it carefully.** Some people put a small piece of opaque tape or a light-dimming sticker over an indicator light. If you try this:

- Read the warnings in your machine's manual first. If it says not to cover the unit, follow that.
- Cover only the indicator light itself, not the area around it.
- Don't put tape over air intakes, vents, or openings, or over the power adapter.
- Don't cover any part that gets warm, especially on battery models while charging.
- Don't drape a cloth or towel over the whole machine.
- Covering vents or warm parts can trap heat, which can lead to overheating and, in rare cases, a fire risk.
- Never cover a smoke detector's light or any part of a smoke detector, even if its light is the one bothering you.

**If the whole room is too bright,** a small LED is probably not the main problem. Light coming in around the window usually matters more. ## FAQ

**Do any white noise machines have no lights at all?**

Some are described that way by the manufacturer. Yogasleep says the Dohm Classic has "No screens or blue light" and describes the Dohm Uno as "No Apps. No Screens," with no night light listed for either. Yogasleep's pages don't mention an indicator light either way, so check the listing photos if even a tiny dot matters to you.

**Can you turn off the light on a Hatch sound machine?**

For the Hatch Restore 3, Hatch's quickstart guide says the clock display can be hidden, and tapping the device shows the time again. Check Hatch's current guide, since instructions can change between models and updates.

**Is it safe to cover a sound machine's light with tape?**

Check your manual's warnings first. If you do it, cover only the indicator light, and keep tape off vents, air openings, the power adapter, and anything that warms up, especially while charging. Don't cover the whole machine with cloth, and never cover a smoke detector.

**Is a dimmable night light enough if I want a dark room?**

It depends on whether "dimmable" includes fully off. Many listings only say the brightness adjusts. Look for wording that says the light can be turned off, or a separate on/off control.

**Is a phone app a no-light alternative?**

It can be, but a phone brings its own light, since the screen can come on with notifications. If you use an app, turning the phone face down and silencing notifications may help. Some people find a separate machine simpler.
