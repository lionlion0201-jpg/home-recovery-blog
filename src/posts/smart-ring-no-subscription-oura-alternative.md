## What "No Subscription" Actually Means for a Smart Ring

"No subscription" sounds simple, but each company defines it a little differently. Before comparing rings, it helps to know what the phrase does and doesn't promise.

### Free Core Features vs. Optional Paid Add-Ons

When a ring maker says "no subscription," it usually means the ring's main features work in the app without a recurring fee. It doesn't necessarily mean every feature the company ever offers will be free.

- **RingConn** says there is "no monthly subscription fee for current RingConn rings" and that "supported core functions are currently available without a recurring subscription fee." RingConn doesn't define exactly which functions count as "core," and the word "currently" leaves room for change.
- **The RingConn Gen 2 Air's Amazon listing** words it as "No Subscription Fee for Standard Features." That's the manufacturer's own phrasing, so we use it as written.
- **Samsung** has said, according to reporting at launch, that current Galaxy Ring features don't require a subscription, but that terms for future Samsung Health features may differ. A Samsung executive has also been quoted in later reports as saying the company is considering a paid tier. We couldn't find a statement about subscriptions on Samsung's U.S. Galaxy Ring page itself, though the Amazon listing title includes "No App Subscription."

The practical takeaway: "no subscription" describes a company's policy today. Policies can change, so read the current wording on the product page before you buy.

### How Oura's Membership Works Today

Oura works differently. The ring is a one-time purchase, and Oura sells a paid membership on top of it.

Oura's support page is direct about what happens without it: "Without an active membership, you'll only be able to see your three daily Oura scores." So you can still see your daily scores for free. What the membership adds, according to Oura, is the deeper detail, such as detailed sleep analysis, 24-hour heart rate, blood oxygen and stress features.

Oura also says that "New members receive one complimentary month of membership." That month starts when you connect the ring to the app, and it can't be paused. Some features need more than 30 days of data, so they may not appear during that first month.

## Oura Ring Alternatives Without Subscription: The Picks

### RingConn Gen 2 and Gen 2 Air: The Most Direct No-Subscription Choice for iPhone or Android

**What they're for:** people who want sleep and activity tracking in a ring, on either an iPhone or an Android phone, without a recurring fee.

RingConn makes two current rings that fit this guide, and the choice between them comes down to three things: one extra sleep feature, a charging case, and the price tier.

**RingConn Gen 2.** RingConn describes its sleep tracking as covering "stages, breathing quality, heart activity, temperature, and trends," and lists "Automatic sleep breathing insights" as a feature. It claims 10–12 days of battery life, and the charging case comes in the box. RingConn says the case can extend total use well beyond a single charge. The Amazon listing describes the app as "[No Subscription Fees]" with lifetime access to its features. The ring is rated IP68, and RingConn lists water resistance to 100 meters.

The Gen 2's product listings make strong claims about its breathing-related features. We're not repeating those claims here. A consumer ring isn't a substitute for a sleep study, and RingConn's own Gen 2 page says: "This device is not a medical device and is not intended for use in the diagnosis, treatment, or prevention of any disease or medical condition." If you're worried about your breathing during sleep, that's a conversation for a doctor, not a ring.

One more note: RingConn's own store showed the Gen 2 as out of stock at the time of writing. On Amazon, the listing we found was sold by RingConn's official store.

**RingConn Gen 2 Air.** The Air sits in a lower price tier. RingConn describes its sleep tracking as covering "stages, heart activity, temperature, and trends." The breathing feature isn't on that list, and RingConn's FAQ confirms the Air doesn't include the Gen 2's sleep breathing feature. RingConn claims up to 10 days of battery life with 90-minute fast charging, and lists compatibility with iOS 17.0+ and Android 10.0+. It's also rated IP68. We didn't find the "not a medical device" statement on the Air's own page, but the same caution applies to any consumer ring.

**Sizing.** Both rings come in sizes 6–14. RingConn offers a free sizing kit when you order from its site, and kits are also listed on Amazon, where the Gen 2 kit and the Gen 2 Air kit are sold as separate items; check that the kit's listing names the ring you plan to buy. RingConn's newer Gen 3 uses a different sizing system, so don't reuse a Gen 2 kit for it.

**Who they may not suit:**

- Anyone who wants Oura-style partner integrations and long-term reports. Check which apps RingConn connects to before you buy.
- People who want a guarantee that every future feature will stay free. RingConn says core functions are free "currently."
- Anyone hoping a ring will tell them whether they have a sleep disorder. Neither ring is a medical device.

### Samsung Galaxy Ring: If You Already Use a Galaxy Phone

**What it's for:** people who already use a Samsung Galaxy phone and want a ring that fits into Samsung Health.

The Galaxy Ring is a conditional pick. The most important limitation is the phone. Samsung's U.S. page says "a Samsung account must be registered on your Samsung Galaxy smartphone," and the Amazon listing says "ANDROID ONLY: Galaxy Ring is compatible with Android smartphones." In short, it won't work with an iPhone. How well it works with Android phones from other brands isn't spelled out the same way on every Samsung page, so check the current product page if you don't use a Galaxy phone.

On sleep, Samsung says the ring can "Analyze and record your sleep by measuring your heart rate, blood oxygen, and skin temperature." Two conditions are worth knowing:

- Snoring detection works only with your phone placed within about two feet of you.
- A footnote on Samsung's page says you must wear the device for 7 nights during a 30-night period; check the product page for which sleep features this applies to.

Samsung claims about seven days per charge, or up to 14 days counting a full charge from the case. Smaller sizes may last less, and Samsung's own note says up to six days for those. It's rated 10ATM and IP68. Samsung says its heart rate features are "not intended for use in the diagnosis of disease or other conditions."

On subscriptions, Samsung said at launch, according to reports, that current features don't require one, while leaving room for future Samsung Health features to come with different terms.

Sizing runs from 5 to 15. The Amazon listing itself tells you to size before you buy and to get the Samsung Galaxy Ring Sizing Kit first. Follow that advice, and make sure the kit you order is Samsung's own.

**Be aware of the ratings.** When we checked, the Galaxy Ring's customer rating on Amazon was noticeably lower than both RingConn models'. Read through the recent reviews on the listing before you decide.

**Who it may not suit:**

- iPhone users. It's Android only.
- Anyone using a non-Samsung Android phone who isn't sure which features will work. Check first.
- People who want written confirmation that features will stay free long term. Samsung's statements leave that open.

## Before You Buy: A 6-Point Smart Ring Checklist

- [ ] **Does it work with your phone?** Galaxy Ring is Android only. RingConn and Oura list both iPhone and Android, but check the minimum OS version on the listing.
- [ ] **What's free, and what's optional?** Read the maker's own wording. "Core functions" or "standard features" being free is not the same as every feature being free.
- [ ] **Have you worn the matching sizing kit for a full day, including overnight?** Kits are generation-specific, and finger size can change through the day. If you're planning to wait for a price drop, it helps to [order the sizing kit before a sale]({{ '/posts/what-to-buy-on-sale-recovery-sleep/' | url }}) so you're ready to choose a size.
- [ ] **Does the claimed battery life match when you can charge?** A ring that needs charging every week or so works best if you have a regular time to top it up.
- [ ] **What's the return window?** Check the terms on the listing you actually buy from, since they can differ from the manufacturer's own store.
- [ ] **Are you hoping it will answer a medical question?** If so, a ring isn't the right tool. See a doctor.

## Who a Smart Ring Isn't For

- **People who want to test for or diagnose a sleep condition.** These rings aren't medical devices. If you're concerned about loud snoring, pauses in breathing or daytime sleepiness, talk to a doctor about proper testing.
- **People who don't like wearing anything to bed.** A ring only tracks sleep if you wear it every night.
- **People who won't look at the app.** A tracker is only useful if you check it. If you don't plan to look at your data most mornings, the ring may end up in a drawer.

## FAQ

**Is there a smart ring with no subscription that works with iPhone?**

Yes. RingConn says its current rings, including the Gen 2 and Gen 2 Air, don't require a monthly fee for core functions, and RingConn lists iOS compatibility. The Samsung Galaxy Ring doesn't work with iPhones.

**Can you use an Oura Ring without a membership?**

Yes, but with limits. Oura's support page says that without an active membership, "you'll only be able to see your three daily Oura scores." Detailed sleep analysis and other features require the paid membership.

**Will subscription-free rings stay free?**

No company can promise that. RingConn says core functions are "currently" free, and Samsung has said future features may come with different terms. Check the current wording on the product page before you buy.

**Do smart rings diagnose sleep apnea?**

No. Consumer smart rings aren't a substitute for a medical diagnosis. RingConn states that the Gen 2 is not a medical device, and Samsung says its heart rate features aren't intended to diagnose any condition. If you're worried about your breathing during sleep, talk to a doctor.

## A Note on Health and Accuracy

The rings in this guide are sold for wellness tracking, not as medical diagnostic devices. RingConn says the Gen 2 "is not a medical device and is not intended for use in the diagnosis, treatment, or prevention of any disease or medical condition." Samsung says the Galaxy Ring's heart rate features are not intended for diagnosis. Oura's terms say its services are for wellness and informational purposes, apart from a limited number of regulated features. Sleep stages and other numbers from a ring are estimates, and different devices can give different results for the same night.
