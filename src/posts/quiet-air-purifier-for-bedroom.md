## What Makes an Air Purifier Hard to Sleep Next To

### Fan Noise, and Why the Change in Sound Matters More Than the Number

For many light sleepers, a steady hum is easier to tune out than a sound that changes. Two common features cause changes:

- **Auto mode.** A sensor raises the fan speed when it detects more particles. At night, the fan can get louder without warning.
- **Power-saving modes that pause the fan.** Coway's manual for the Airmega Mighty AP-1512HH says that in Eco mode, the fan stops on its own when no pollution is detected for 30 minutes.

We apply the same idea in our guide to [quiet space heaters for light sleepers]({{ '/posts/quiet-space-heaters-bedroom-light-sleepers/' | url }}), where clicks and cycling can bother people more than a constant hum.

### Display Lights, Air-Quality Rings, and Filter Reminders

Light on an air purifier usually comes from one of four places: power and button lights, an air-quality indicator (often a colored ring and frequently the brightest light on the unit), a filter replacement light, and on some models a screen. Check each one separately. And remember that "dimmed" and "off" are different. A listing that says the display dims at night doesn't tell you it goes fully dark.

### A Note on Ionizers and Ozone

According to the EPA's guide to air cleaners in the home, you should avoid air cleaners that intentionally produce ozone, a lung irritant. The EPA notes that some electronic air cleaners, including ionizers, may produce ozone in some cases, and that no federal agency has approved ozone generators for use in occupied spaces.

That doesn't make every purifier with an ionizer a problem. Since 2010, the California Air Resources Board (CARB) has required portable indoor air cleaners sold in California to be certified. Electronic models must meet an ozone emission limit of 0.050 parts per million, and CARB publishes a list of certified devices marked "Mechanical" or "Electronic." For an Electronic model, certification means it met that limit, not that it produces zero ozone. Two things to check: whether the model is on CARB's list, and whether you can switch the ionizer off.

## Quiet vs. Powerful: The Trade-Off on Every Purifier

Listings tend to skip this part. The EPA explains that a purifier's Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) is usually measured at the highest fan speed, and that a lower fan speed filters less air.

So the setting quiet enough to sleep next to is usually not the setting the room-size claim is based on. Two common ways to work within that:

- **Choose a purifier rated for a room somewhat larger than yours,** then run it at a lower speed. The catch is a bigger unit to find space for.
- **Run it higher before bed, then switch to the quietest setting** when you lie down.

### How to Read a dB Figure on a Listing

Many manufacturers publish a noise range, like "22–54.5 dB." Treat it as a rough idea only:

- **Measurement conditions usually aren't listed.** None of the manufacturer sources we checked said how far from the unit the noise was measured.
- **You can't compare brands directly.** Without the same setup, a lower number from one brand doesn't prove that unit is quieter.
- **The low number is usually the lowest setting,** which also filters the least air.

For scale, the EPA says roughly 50 dB is about the sound of a modern refrigerator, and suggests looking at models with lower published noise levels if you think noise would stop you from using one.

### Matching CADR to Your Bedroom Size

The EPA's rule of thumb for particles, assuming an 8-foot ceiling:

| Room size (8-ft ceiling) | Suggested minimum CADR (cfm) |
|---|---|
| 100 sq ft | 65 |
| 200 sq ft | 130 |
| 300 sq ft | 195 |
| 400 sq ft | 260 |

With a higher ceiling, leave extra margin. CADR measures particles only; the EPA notes there's no widely used rating for removing gases or odors.

## How to Run an Air Purifier in Your Bedroom Without It Waking You

### Run It on High Before Bed, Then Drop to Sleep Mode

The EPA notes that a higher fan speed, or a longer run time, filters more air. One routine: run it on a higher setting with the door closed while you get ready for bed, then switch to Sleep Mode, Night mode, or the lowest speed when you lie down.

### Placement

The EPA suggests placing a purifier in the room where you spend the most time, which for many people is the bedroom. For comfort, put it a few feet from the bed, point the outlet away from your face, and follow the manufacturer's clearance guidance so the intake and outlet aren't blocked.

### Taming Leftover Glow

Start with the built-in settings: Display Off or Sleep Mode on the Levoit, the long press on Coway's IONIZER button, or Night mode on the Blueair. If a small light remains, turn the lit side away from the bed. If you consider tape, read your manual's warnings first and never cover sensors, intakes, or outlets. Our guide on [how to cut the glow from a sound machine]({{ '/posts/white-noise-machine-without-light/' | url }}) has more safe options.

### Keep the Filter on Schedule

Levoit and Coway both suggest cleaning the pre-filter every 2–4 weeks. Set a reminder rather than waiting for the indicator light.

## FAQ

**Is a quiet air purifier quiet enough to sleep next to?**

It depends on the unit, the setting, and the person. Published dB ranges give a rough idea but can't be compared across brands without measurement conditions.

**Can I turn off the lights on an air purifier?**

It depends on the model. The Levoit Core 300S-P's Display Off button turns off its lights except the filter check light. A long press on the Coway AP-1512HH's IONIZER button turns the air-quality light off. Blueair describes the 311i Max's Night mode as dimming the lights.

**Do air purifiers with ionizers produce ozone?**

The EPA says some ionizers may produce ozone. Models CARB lists as Electronic have met California's 0.050 ppm limit, which isn't the same as zero.

**Should I leave my air purifier on all night?**

Follow your model's manual. Timers can help: the Coway has 1, 4, and 8-hour options, and Levoit offers schedules in its app.

**Is an air purifier the same as a humidifier?**

No. A purifier filters the air; a humidifier adds moisture to it.
