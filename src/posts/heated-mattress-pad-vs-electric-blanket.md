## Heated Mattress Pad vs. Electric Blanket: The Short Answer

- **Cold sheets are the main problem, and you want to keep your usual comforter:** a heated mattress pad is likely the better fit.
- **You want warmth on the couch too, or only before bed:** an electric blanket may suit you. Keep it to the bed and couch; Sunbeam's manual warns against using some heated products, such as throws, in rocking chairs or recliners.
- **You have trouble feeling heat on your skin, or can't easily work the controller:** neither one may be for you. Read "Who Shouldn't Use a Heated Mattress Pad or Electric Blanket" below first.

Sunbeam, which makes both, frames the difference on its comparison page as the direction the heat comes from: the pad stays in one place and warms the bed from below through the night. That's marketing, not a safety promise, but it's a useful starting point.

## Where the Heat Comes From, and Why It Matters

### A heated mattress pad warms you from below

A heated mattress pad goes between your mattress and fitted sheet and stays there. Your sheet and comforter go on as usual, the way the setup instructions show.

Sunbeam's manual notes that a heated mattress pad is not a heating pad, so it won't feel hot to the touch when lying flat. Gentler than expected isn't automatically a fault; if you suspect a problem, follow the manual's checks.

### An electric blanket warms you from above

An electric blanket goes on top of you, so it's portable: the bed tonight, the couch tomorrow.

The trade-off is what goes over it. CPSC's home safety checklist (written for older adults, but general advice) says not to cover an electric blanket in use with other blankets or comforters, not to let pets sleep on it, not to tuck it in, and to let the edges hang off the sides and foot of the bed, because trapped heat can be a fire risk.

That's worth knowing before you choose. Many people picture an electric blanket under a thick winter comforter, and CPSC's guidance doesn't fit that picture. If you want your heavy comforter on top, a heated mattress pad lines up better.

### Folding, bunching, and tucking

Both types share one rule. Sunbeam's manual warns that folding or bunching heated bedding can cause it to overheat while in use, and says not to tuck in the heated area. CPSC and UL, the safety certification organization, both say not to fold an electric blanket while it's on.

For a pad, smooth out folds each time you make the bed. For a blanket, keep it spread flat while it's on.

## Can You Sleep With a Heated Mattress Pad On? What the Manuals Say

We'll stick to what the manuals and safety organizations put in writing.

| Product | What the manual or maker says about overnight use | Auto-off |
|---|---|---|
| Sunbeam (pad and blanket) | The heated bedding manual we read doesn't say not to use it while sleeping, and doesn't say you can. It describes the auto-off as an energy-saving feature | 10 hours; some models let you disable the auto-off |
| Serta Plush Heated Blanket | We couldn't get the manual. The product page lists the timer only | 1 to 12 hours, selectable |

**Sunbeam.** The manual we read is Sunbeam's general heated bedding manual (blankets, pads, and throws), not one for a specific model. It has no line saying not to use the products while sleeping, and no line saying overnight use is fine. It says the auto-off switches a blanket or pad off after 10 hours of continuous use, to save energy, not as a safety feature. It tells you to switch the product off when not in use, and notes some models let you disable the auto-off (the display shows "StayOn"). Sunbeam's comparison page describes the pad as warming you through the night, so the maker expects overnight use. Your own manual comes first.

**Serta.** We couldn't find the manual. The product page confirms the 1-to-12-hour timer. Read the manual that comes with it before deciding how to use it overnight.

**Electric blankets.** CPSC says nothing should go on top of one while it's in use. UL recommends a more cautious routine: warm the bed first, then switch the blanket off before you go to sleep.

**Heating pads are a different product.** A therapeutic heating pad, the kind for a sore back, is not a heated mattress pad. CPSC says to turn heating pads off before sleep, because they can cause serious burns even on low settings. Don't use one in the bed as a stand-in. If only your feet are cold, our guide to [foot warmers for bed]({{ '/posts/foot-warmer-for-bed/' | url }}) covers those options.

**The most cautious approach** is UL's: preheat the bed, then turn it off before you go to sleep. We're not telling you to use heated bedding all night, or never to. Follow your manual.

## Before You Plug It In: A Heated Bedding Checklist

- [ ] Check the label for a UL or ETL mark. UL recommends using products certified by a nationally recognized testing lab.
- [ ] Look up the brand and model number at cpsc.gov/Recalls. For the recalled Sunbeam blanket, CPSC says the model number is on the wash label.
- [ ] Don't use a heated mattress pad and an electric blanket at the same time (Sunbeam manual).

- [ ] Keep a heated mattress pad flat. Smooth out folds and bunching every time (Sunbeam manual).
- [ ] Don't run the cord between the mattress and box spring, and don't use an extension cord (Sunbeam manual).
- [ ] Check the bedding, cord, and controller for damage before each use (Sunbeam manual).
- [ ] Don't use it on an adjustable bed, waterbed, or bunk bed (Sunbeam manual).
- [ ] After washing, don't plug it in until it's completely dry (Sunbeam manual).

## Who Shouldn't Use a Heated Mattress Pad or Electric Blanket

Sunbeam's heated bedding manual says its products should not be used by, or for:

- Infants or young children
- People who are helpless or can't move easily
- People with paraplegia or quadriplegia
- People with diabetes
- People with poor circulation or reduced ability to feel heat
- People who can't understand the instructions or operate the controller

UL likewise doesn't recommend electric blankets for infants, young children, or anyone who is paralyzed or can't understand how to operate them safely.

A few more points from the manual:

- **Pacemakers and other medical devices:** talk to your doctor before using heated bedding.
- **Pain-relief creams and ointments:** don't use heated bedding on nights you've applied them, because of the risk of burns.

## FAQ

### Can you sleep with a heated mattress pad on?

Sunbeam's heated bedding manual neither forbids nor explicitly allows overnight use. It describes the 10-hour auto-off as energy-saving, not a safety feature. Follow your model's manual. The most cautious routine, which UL recommends, is to preheat, then switch it off before you go to sleep.

### Is it safe to use a heated mattress pad and an electric blanket together?

Sunbeam's manual says not to use its heated bedding with any other electrically heated product. Pick one or the other.

### Can I put a comforter on top of an electric blanket?

CPSC says not to cover an electric blanket with other blankets or comforters while it's in use. If you want a comforter on top, a heated mattress pad fits that routine better.

### Can you wash a heated mattress pad?

All three picks are listed as machine washable. Sunbeam's manual calls for cold water on a gentle cycle, low drying, removing it while still damp, and not plugging it in until completely dry. Don't wash the controller.

### Do I need a dual-control heated mattress pad if I sleep alone?

Usually not. In the products we checked, Twin sizes come with a single control, and some Queen and larger models have dual controls. For two people with different temperature needs, see our guide to [dual-zone options for couples]({{ '/posts/couples-different-sleep-temperatures-dual-zone-bed/' | url }}).
