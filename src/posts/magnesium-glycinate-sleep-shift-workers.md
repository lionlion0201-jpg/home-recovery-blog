## Why Shift Work Disrupts Sleep — and Where Magnesium Fits In

### The Circadian Challenge of Rotating or Night Shifts

Your circadian rhythm runs on light exposure, meal timing, and routine — three things shift work actively scrambles. Sleeping during daylight hours, rotating between day and night shifts, and eating at irregular times all push against the body's internal clock, which is part of why shift workers report shorter, more fragmented sleep than daytime workers on average.

### What Magnesium Glycinate Is (and How It's Different From Other Forms)

Magnesium glycinate pairs magnesium with glycine, an amino acid that has its own calming associations. Compared to magnesium citrate or oxide — forms more commonly used for digestive purposes — glycinate is generally considered gentler on the stomach and is the form most often marketed specifically for sleep and relaxation.

## What the Research Suggests About Magnesium Glycinate and Sleep

### What Current Evidence Says (and Doesn't Say)

Some research links adequate magnesium levels to better sleep quality, and small studies have explored magnesium supplementation for sleep in older adults and people with mild deficiency. The evidence specific to shift workers, however, is limited, and most existing studies aren't large or long enough to draw firm conclusions. Magnesium is not a studied treatment for circadian rhythm disorders.

### Why "May Support" Is the Right Way to Think About It

Given the current state of research, "may support relaxation" is a more accurate framing than any promise of better sleep. For shift workers specifically, magnesium is best thought of as one small piece alongside light management, consistent sleep windows, and a wind-down routine — not a replacement for any of them.

### Capsule vs. Powder vs. Gummy — Which Fits a Shift Work Routine?

Capsules travel well and are easiest to take on an inconsistent schedule — useful if your "bedtime" shifts by 12 hours depending on the week. Powders mix into a bedtime drink, which some people find easier to build into a wind-down ritual, but they require more prep during a rushed morning-after-nightshift routine. Gummies are the most convenient grab-and-go option but often contain less magnesium per serving and more added sugar, worth checking on the label.

### What to Look for in a Subscription (Flexibility, Pause Options, Trial Periods)

## Who Should Not Take Magnesium Supplements Without Talking to a Doctor First

Magnesium supplements aren't appropriate for everyone to start without medical guidance. This includes anyone who is pregnant or nursing, has a kidney condition (magnesium is cleared by the kidneys, and impaired function can lead to buildup), or takes medications such as diuretics, antibiotics, or osteoporosis drugs that can interact with magnesium. If any of this applies to you, check with a doctor or pharmacist before adding a supplement, regardless of format or brand.
