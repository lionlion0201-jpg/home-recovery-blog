## Why Hotel Rooms Are Hard to Sleep In on Away Trips

At home, you've stopped noticing your bedroom's sounds and light leaks. A hotel room hands you a new set every trip.

### Hallway Doors, Ice Machines, and HVAC Cycling

Most hotel noise is sudden and uneven: a heavy door closing on its own, voices passing at midnight, an elevator chime, a cart rolling by. The room's heating and cooling unit adds a second problem, because it switches on and off through the night instead of running at a steady level.

A sound machine doesn't block any of this. It plays a steady sound that makes sudden noises stand out less, which some people find easier to sleep through. For the longer explanation of masking, see [our guide to noise machines for light sleepers]({{ '/posts/white-noise-machines-vs-sound-machines-light-sleepers/' | url }}).

### Curtain Gaps, Door Cracks, and Standby Lights

Hotel curtains often don't meet in the middle. There's usually a bright gap under the door, and the room is full of small lights: the TV standby dot, the thermostat display, the smoke detector, the alarm clock. Together they can keep a room from ever getting fully dark.

### What Tournament Weekends Add: Shared Rooms and Early Call Times

Team travel and youth tournaments make all of this harder. You may be sharing a room with a teammate, a coach, or your own kids, and not everyone goes to bed at the same time. Call times can be early, and a hotel full of other teams can mean hallway traffic late into the evening. A small kit won't fix all of this, but it gives you more control over the parts you can change.

## What to Look for in a Portable White Noise Machine for Hotel Travel

A portable white noise machine for hotel rooms has a different job than a bedside unit at home. Here's the checklist we used when we compared models:

- **A built-in battery.** Hotel outlets aren't reliably next to the bed. A rechargeable machine can sit wherever you need it, with no cord across the room.
- **The charging port.** If a machine charges over USB-C, it can share a cable with your phone or other gear. Some machines come with a cable but no wall adapter, so check the listing and pack a USB power adapter if one isn't included.
- **The type of sound.** Hotel heating and cooling units tend to make a low hum. Some people find fan or brown noise sounds less jarring against that kind of hum than a bright, hissy white noise.
- **Volume headroom.** A machine that tops out too quietly won't do much in a loud hallway. It helps to have some room above the level you actually use.
- **Timer or all-night play.** Some people like the sound to shut off after they fall asleep; others prefer it running until morning so a 3 a.m. door slam doesn't wake them.
- **Light on the unit.** A night light is handy with kids in the room, but it works against a fully dark room.
- **Size, weight, and a clip or strap.** Gear bags fill up fast. A strap or clip lets a small unit hang from a bag, headboard, or bed frame.
- **Bluetooth.** Playing your own audio is convenient, but it's also a reason to keep your phone in bed.
- **Whether you'll still hear your alarm.** A sound machine right next to your head can drown out a phone alarm or a knock on the door. Placement matters; see the checklist below.

## Hotel Sleep Kit Checklist for Away Games and Tournaments

**What to pack:**

- [ ] Portable sound machine, plus its charging cable
- [ ] A USB power adapter, in case your machine doesn't include one
- [ ] A portable blackout shade or a contoured sleep mask
- [ ] A couple of binder clips or clothespins to pin the curtain gap shut
- [ ] A small roll of painter's tape to cover standby lights
- [ ] Reusable earplugs
- [ ] Optional: a compact massage gun and its charger (check the TSA's and your airline's current rules on lithium-ion batteries if you're flying)

**When you check in:**

- [ ] Ask for a room away from the elevator and the ice machine.
- [ ] Place the sound machine between the bed and the door, not right next to your head, so you'll still hear your alarm.
- [ ] Set the air unit's fan to run continuously if the thermostat allows it.
- [ ] Clip the curtains, tape over the brightest standby lights (never cover a smoke detector), and put a towel along the bottom of the door.
- [ ] With the sound machine running, play your alarm once at its normal volume to make sure you can hear it.

## Who This Kit Isn't For

This kit isn't the right call for everyone:

- **People who sleep fine on the road.** There's no reason to add gear to your bag.
- **People who need to hear what's happening at night.** Parents rooming with small children, and coaches or chaperones who may be called on during the night, should keep the volume low or skip masking sound entirely.
- **Kids and people with sensitive hearing.** Follow each manufacturer's guidance on volume and placement, and keep the machine away from the bed.
- **People whose sleep trouble doesn't go away at home.** If you have trouble sleeping most nights, not just on trips, talk to a doctor. A sound machine isn't a treatment for a sleep disorder.
- **People who shouldn't use a massage gun.** If any of the warnings above apply, leave it out.
- **Ultralight packers.** Earplugs and a sleep mask cover much of what this kit does and don't need charging.

## FAQ

**Can a white noise machine help athletes sleep before a game?**

A white noise machine can make sudden hotel noises less noticeable, and some people find that makes it easier to fall asleep in an unfamiliar room. We can't say it will affect your sleep quality or performance.

**Can I bring a portable white noise machine on a plane?**

All three picks have a built-in rechargeable battery, and devices like these commonly use lithium-ion cells. Rules are set by the TSA and your airline and can change, so check the TSA's "What Can I Bring?" tool and your airline's current battery policy before you pack.

**How loud should it be in a hotel room?**

Use the lowest volume that takes the edge off hallway noise. Follow the manufacturer's guidance on volume and distance, and place the machine away from your head.

**Is a phone app enough?**

It can be. A white noise app is a free way to find out whether masking sound helps you at all. The tradeoffs: your phone stays on and nearby all night, and it competes with your alarm.

**Should kids use one at tournaments?**

Some families use them, and the Hushh 2 is designed with young children in mind. Follow the manufacturer's guidance on age, volume, and distance from the bed, and talk to your pediatrician if you have questions.
